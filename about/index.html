About Mortal League

Community structure for a world worth returning to.

Mortal League is an independent collection of league, guild, safety, and planning tools for Mortal Online 1 and Mortal Online 2. It is built around a simple idea: an established game world can stay active when players have dependable ways to organize themselves.

“

Think of it like a local softball league. The field already exists. What keeps people returning is the organization around it.

The purpose

Make more of the world accessible.

01

Help players find people

Guild discovery, applications, Sherpa service, and standing records reduce the friction between a player and the community they need.

02

Create repeatable play

Seasons, ladders, territory scores, war records, and shared rules give groups reasons to organize and return.

03

Preserve community knowledge

Planning tools, rulings, public feedback, and curated records keep useful information from disappearing into private chats.

04

Keep boundaries visible

Public pages, private reports, moderation, consent, and official support routes must remain clearly separated.

Operating boundaries

Clear claims. Clear control.

Independent

Mortal League is a community project. Star Vault does not operate or approve this Beta, and the site does not speak for Star Vault.

Beta

Rules and tools may change as the community tests them. Wagers remain disabled during Beta.

Privacy first

Private evidence, rejected applications, contact details, and safety reports do not belong on public boards.

No substitute for support

Game accounts, cheating, security, harassment, and technical support must use the appropriate official or private route.

Long-term stewardship

Built to be governed, not to become a bottleneck.

The long-term version needs secure hosting, named moderators, published policies, appeals, backups, and accountable data control. If Star Vault ever reviews or adopts the project, it should control the domain, rules, seasons, privacy, security, referees, sanctions, appeals, and final decisions. Community volunteers can supply labor and maintenance inside those boundaries.

Until then, connected services remain limited. Pages say when a tool is public, local-only, planned, or not connected.

About Mortal League

Community structure for a world worth returning to.

Mortal League is an independent collection of league, guild, safety, and planning tools for Mortal Online 1 and Mortal Online 2. It is built around a simple idea: an established game world can stay active when players have dependable ways to organize themselves.

The purpose

Make more of the world accessible.

01

Guild discovery, applications, Sherpa service, and standing records reduce the friction between a player and the community they need.

02

Seasons, ladders, territory scores, war records, and shared rules give groups reasons to organize and return.

03

Preserve community knowledge

Planning tools, rulings, public feedback, and curated records keep useful information from disappearing into private chats.

04

Keep boundaries visible

Public pages, private reports, moderation, consent, and official support routes must remain clearly separated.

The position

Player organizing is necessary to realize the franchise's stated social vision. The League is one voluntary proposal for making that work durable across guild boundaries.

Download statement Read the source record

The MO League holds that player organizing is necessary to realize Mortal Online’s stated social vision. A world built around trade, territorial conflict, reputation, and cooperation requires people who recruit, negotiate, teach, supply, host, and keep agreements. Guilds, alliances, trading associations, community events, and leagues are forms that work takes. The League’s particular structure is our proposal for carrying it further.

On October 7, 2008, Mats Persson described Mortal Online through personal skill and interaction. His examples included miners seeking protection, guilds trading or going to war, and new characters contributing to play with friends. Collective activity already formed part of the franchise’s public design before the first game launched. [1]

On April 2, 2009, Star Vault explained its intention to encourage social life by “forcing” people to communicate. Players would arrange auctions and find companions in gathering places. Limits on individual skills would preserve cooperation. Parties would negotiate loot, with the possibility of betrayal. New players would contribute to guild wars and shared encounters early in their development. Each example assigns players a practical social responsibility: finding people, arranging terms, and acting together. [2]

On September 7, 2009, Persson identified player interaction as a test for design decisions. He described guild membership, promotion, titles, shared storage, and access rights; group training and caravan protection; and consequences administered by players alongside the game’s flagging system. Specialized craftspeople would earn recognition through their work. These statements justify player institutions with responsibilities, procedures, and public standing. [3] [4]

An undated prelaunch FAQ, preserved in a community reproduction, links trade, learning, rumors, and alliances to communication. It describes guilds needing complementary characters and a sandbox whose activities and outcomes players help create. Its promised mechanics belong to the historical record; its organizing principle is interdependence. [5]

In 2012, Henrik Nyström explained that combatants depended on other playstyles for economic life and additional activities. [6]

The surviving official MO1 website describes allies building keeps and pursuing political control through power, violence, or diplomacy. Its Steam description adds collective city construction and siege warfare. These descriptions give player organization economic, military, and political scope. Their current wording has no verified original publication date. [7] [8]

On June 6, 2020, Nyström carried the social premise into Mortal Online 2, identifying player interaction as a core pillar and player activity as a source of content lasting years. He also emphasized the first hours of a newcomer’s experience. For the League, that connects long-term community work with immediate entry: a new player needs a route to useful participation. [9]

Players have already undertaken this work. On February 18, 2021, MÝRVIKINGS announced a Champions Tournament coordinated through Chronicles of Nave, with published rules and a public bracket. On August 30, 2021, Events advertised an open community hub combining job boards, training, contractors, caravans, and event staff. The League recognizes these precedents. Discord has supported substantial organizing, alongside guilds, forums, and external tools. The available record does not establish that a comparable organization has never existed. [10] [11]

Developer involvement has continued alongside player initiative. On April 13, 2022, Star Vault staff introduced Community Scenarios to create situations players would encounter, interpret, and resolve, with outcomes recorded publicly. On February 18, 2026, Star Vault explicitly invited player-run events at Tindrem Arena while also planning official events and automated features. February 26 test notes described arena services supporting those events. The March 7 Grand Gathering announcement invited conversation, dueling, and the sharing of crafted goods. [12] [13] [14] [15]

The current MO2 homepage gives players responsibility for settlements, trade routes, war, peace, and protection. Its FAQ emphasizes individual reputation, guilds, political power, housing, and regional trade. Across these statements, players inhabit a world whose social relations they continually produce through their actions. [16] [17]

We therefore understand organizing as a necessary contribution to the franchise’s success on its own stated terms: a populated world of consequential relationships and shared activity. That is the League’s conclusion from the developer record. A particular league format has to earn participation and demonstrate its usefulness through practice.

The League aims to make organizing durable across guild boundaries. Public rules, event records, standings, evidence, and appeals allow people to discover opportunities, assess commitments, and revisit decisions after a chat has moved on. Guild finding and training help newcomers contribute. Reputation records should recognize the supply, escort, teaching, and diplomatic work that sustains shared play.

Star Vault supplies the world, mechanics, services, and official activities. Players supply the independent associations, commitments, and trust through which a player society takes shape. A developer-created feature provides possibilities; people still have to choose an undertaking, gather collaborators, and carry it out. The League accepts responsibility for that work.

Participation remains voluntary. Guilds retain their identities and priorities, and players remain free to create other institutions and events. League authority extends to its participating activities under its published rules. Star Vault retains authority over the game and its policies. This is an official MO League statement of purpose, with developer sources documenting the franchise’s vision; it claims no Star Vault endorsement of the League.

Our tools should reduce the administrative burden of organizing while preserving human decisions. Referees must review contested evidence and account for their rulings. Organizers should spend less time repeating forms and more time arranging activity with players. We will judge the League by the opportunities it produces, the contributions it recognizes, and the worthwhile time people spend together in Mortal Online.

Operating boundaries

Clear claims. Clear control.

Independent

Mortal League is a community project. Star Vault does not operate or approve this Beta, and the site does not speak for Star Vault. Written permission has not been received, and public availability does not establish that permission is unnecessary.

Beta

Rules and tools may change as the community tests them. Wagers remain disabled during Beta.

Privacy first

Private evidence, rejected applications, contact details, and safety reports do not belong on public boards.

No substitute for support

Game accounts, cheating, security, harassment, and technical support must use the appropriate official or private route.

Long-term stewardship

Built to be governed, not to become a bottleneck.

The long-term version needs secure hosting, named moderators, published policies, appeals, backups, and accountable data control. If Star Vault ever reviews or adopts the project, it should control the domain, rules, seasons, privacy, security, referees, sanctions, appeals, and final decisions. Community volunteers can supply labor and maintenance inside those boundaries.

Until then, connected services remain limited. Pages say when a tool is public, local-only, planned, or not connected.
